# KIROHACKS2026

Idea 1 — Drift (Human-Centered)
A relationship health tracker for friendships, not romance. You add people you care about, set how often you want to connect with them, and it quietly tracks drift — when you last talked, how the conversation felt, whether the gap is growing. When you haven't reached out in a while, it surfaces a specific, personal nudge: not "text Alex," but "Alex mentioned their job interview 3 weeks ago — they probably have news." It generates the opening message for you. No social media, no public graph — pure private relationship memory. This directly addresses the loneliness epidemic with a real ML pattern-recognition layer underneath (sentiment from conversation notes, drift scoring, recency decay). Atharv built a generative storywriter for fun — he will love a system that generates context-aware human communication.
Idea 2 — Triage (Human-Centered)
An emergency room wait-time explainer and patient advocate. ERs are terrifying and opaque — you sit for 4 hours with no idea what's happening or why others are being seen first. Triage takes your symptoms, explains the triage system (ESI levels), tells you roughly where you are in the queue, prepares the exact questions to ask the doctor, and translates the medical jargon in your discharge papers after. The ML angle: a symptom-severity classifier trained on ESI triage categories that scores your situation and explains the reasoning. Nikhil Singh built a Parkinson's diagnostic ML tool from scratch — he will immediately recognize this as real clinical ML, not a toy. Demo is visceral: show a confused patient walking in vs. one who is informed and calm.
Idea 3 — Echo (Human-Centered)
A grief and loss support companion — but specifically for the practical side: the 72 hours of decisions after a death. When someone dies, surviving family members have to make 50+ decisions in a fog of shock: funeral home, death certificate, employer notification, account closure, insurance claims, obituary. Echo walks through every decision in priority order, in plain language, with no sales pressure — and crucially, it remembers where you are so you can come back. The ML layer: a decision-graph engine that reorders steps based on what you've done, your state (estate size, relationship to deceased), and time pressure. This is the highest-uniqueness idea on this list. No one is building this. Karthik will notice the sophisticated state machine. Nikhil and Atharv will respond to the impact.
Idea 4 — Signal (Human-Centered)
A personal symptom journal with pattern detection. Most people have vague recurring symptoms they never figure out — afternoon energy crashes, headaches every Tuesday, joint pain after certain foods. Signal lets you log symptoms in plain language over time ("bad headache, 3pm, rating 7/10, hadn't slept well"), and uses ML to find patterns you can't see: time-of-day correlations, food/activity triggers, weather dependencies. After 2 weeks it surfaces a visual pattern report you can bring to your doctor. The ML pipeline is real — time-series anomaly detection, correlation scoring across tagged variables. Leo spent time building recommendation systems with feedback loops; this is exactly that architecture applied to health. Silu will recognize the model serving layer underneath.
Idea 5 — Mirror (Human-Centered)
A communication style analyzer for workplace relationships. You paste recent Slack/email exchanges with a colleague or manager, and Mirror identifies your communication patterns: do you over-apologize? Do you bury the ask at the end? Are you being passive-aggressive without realizing it? It shows you the gap between what you intend to say and how it reads to the other person, then offers a rewritten version that's clearer without losing your voice. The ML layer: fine-tuned sentiment and pragmatics classification — not just "positive/negative" but "hedging," "assertive," "deferential," "ambiguous." Atharv migrated a 1,000 QPS service and cares about real systems. This has a real NLP model at its core, not just prompting.
